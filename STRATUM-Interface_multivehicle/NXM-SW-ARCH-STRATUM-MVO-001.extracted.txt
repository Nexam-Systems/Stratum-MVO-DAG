1 Purpose, scope and definitions
An architecture is a set of decisions. Everything else is consequence. This document records nine decisions for
coordinating three Dagger air vehicles onto a standoff ring, the evidence from both code baselines that forced
them, and the five rulings still outstanding.
1.1 Purpose
This document defines the architecture of a multi-vehicle orchestration subsystem residing inside STRATUM-Desktop. The
subsystem coordinates up to three Dagger air vehicles onto distinct stations on a standoff ring about a single operator-
designated target. It is written to be reviewed, argued with, and then built from; §11 lists the questions that must be answered
before construction begins.
1.2 Scope
In scope: target designation, ring definition, slot assignment, launch and transit sequencing, arrival detection, separation
assurance, and degraded-mode behaviour for a three-vehicle ensemble. Out of scope, deliberately and explicitly: multiple
simultaneous targets; fixed-wing standoff geometry; inter-vehicle datalinks; and — pending the ruling in §11.5 — any
transition from an orchestrated standoff into a terminal engagement.
1.3 Deﬁnitions
Terms are fixed here and used with these meanings throughout. A term defined here is never used loosely elsewhere in the
document.
Term Definition
Target A single geodetic point designated by the operator. One per mission. Primitive.
Standoﬀ ring The horizontal circle of radius R centred on the target at height H above home. Derived,
never stored independently — it therefore cannot disagree with the target.
Ring slot A station on the ring, identiﬁed by bearing θ from target to hold point (0 = North, clockwise).
One vehicle per slot; one slot per vehicle.

2 The as-built baseline
An architecture built on an assumed baseline is a hypothesis. This section records what the two code baselines actually do, with
references, because four of the nine decisions in §3 were changed by what the scan found.
2.1 The single-vehicle control chain
The essential division of labour in the existing system is already correct, and it is not the one an outside reader would assume.
STRATUM does not compute the standoff hold point. It transmits the target plus three scalars; the firmware translates the
target, resolves the hold altitude against home, and computes the facing yaw itself.

2.2 The ﬁrmware's geometry and staging
The Standoff mode decomposes the manoeuvre into four ordered phases rather than commanding one diagonal move. The
rationale in the firmware documentation is sensor settling: a payload that must acquire the target benefits from turning,
translating and changing height as discrete, observable steps. That decision, taken for a single vehicle and for a wholly
different reason, is what makes vertical deconfliction free for three vehicles — see finding F3 and §7.3.

2.5 Five ﬁndings that dictate the architecture
FINDING F1 — PX4 OWNS THE HOLD-POINT GEOMETRY
On the wire, a three-vehicle ring mission is three single-vehicle standoffs that differ in param2 alone. This is the cheapest
possible extension of a proven contract, and it removes the trajectory generator from STRATUM's requirements entirely.
FINDING F2 — ARRIVAL IS ALREADY OBSERVABLE
standoff.cpp:139 calls request_hold_mode() exactly once when the terminal pose settles. The vehicle therefore
announces its own arrival as a nav_state 9 → 3 transition. No new telemetry, no vehicle-side change, no arrival estimator.
FINDING F3 — THE RUN-IN FLIES AT THE ALTITUDE LATCHED ON ACTIVATION
standoff.cpp:238 latches _approach_alt_amsl from the current global position at begin_sequence(), and
APPROACH publishes that altitude. Climb each vehicle to its own transit level before commit and the run-ins are vertically
separated with zero firmware change. Conditional on STDF_SEQ ∈ {1,3}.
FINDING F4 — CRUISING SPEED IS TRIMMABLE, BUT ONLY AFTER ACTIVATION
on_activation calls reset_cruising_speed(), and APPROACH republishes its setpoint every cycle carrying
cruising_speed = get_cruising_speed() (standoff.cpp:388). A DO_CHANGE_SPEED sent after nav_state ==
9 is confirmed takes effect within one navigator cycle. Sent before, it is discarded.
FINDING F5 — FAILSAFE DEFAULTS ARE HOSTILE TO A RING
No custom mode has bespoke failsafe treatment; all four are bucketed with stock AUTO modes under the generic AutoModes
exception bit (failsafe.cpp:504–542). With stock parameters: NAV_DLL_ACT = 0 — datalink loss triggers nothing;
COM_RCL_EXCEPT = 0 with NAV_RCL_ACT = 2 — RC loss triggers RTL. Three vehicles executing independent,
uncoordinated RTLs from three points on a ring is precisely the crossing pattern this subsystem exists to prevent.

3 Architectural decisions
Nine decisions. Each states the option space, the choice, and the consequence the choice imposes. A decision without a stated
consequence is a preference, not an architecture.
D1 LOCUS OF COORDINATION
Options. (a) ground-side orchestration in STRATUM; (b) onboard distributed coordination between vehicles; (c) hybrid —
ground planning, onboard deconfliction.
Decision: (a). STRATUM plans and supervises; PX4 executes. No inter-vehicle link, no onboard awareness of
neighbours.
Rationale. Option (b) requires an inter-vehicle datalink, a shared world model and a distributed-consensus safety case;
that is a programme, not a feature, and it is unjustified at n = 3. Option (c) requires firmware change to a stack whose
own documentation records that it was statically reviewed but never compile-verified in the environment that produced
it. Option (a) reuses a proven contract end to end.
Consequence. Ground-side coordination promotes the C2 link from a supervisory element to a mission-critical one.
That consequence is paid off by D4: if separation is guaranteed by geometry rather than by supervision, losing the link
degrades the mission without endangering the airframes. The link must not be load-bearing for safety. This is the
single most important structural commitment in this document, and every later decision is checked against it.
D2 COMMAND PRIMITIVE
Options. (a) reuse DO_STANDOFF (31010) + DO_SET_MODE per vehicle; (b) upload per-vehicle missions through
MissionManager; (c) stream offboard setpoints from STRATUM.
Decision: (a), with (b) held in reserve for multi-leg routing.
Option (c) is rejected outright: streaming setpoints from a ground station makes the radio link an inner control loop,
which fails D1's payoff condition immediately and converts every link dropout into a control-loop failure. Option (b) is
over-engineering for a straight-line run-in, but the machinery exists, is already per-vehicle, and remains available if
terrain or airspace ever forces routed approaches.
D3 GEOMETRY BOUNDARY
Decision: STRATUM owns ring geometry and scheduling. PX4 owns hold-point geometry and approach staging.
The boundary is the existing 31010 contract, unchanged.
STRATUM computes the ring from (target, R, H); the per-slot bearing θi; the per-vehicle transit level; and the commit
schedule. It sends (target, R, θi, H) to vehicle i. Nothing else crosses the boundary.
Consequence. The wire contract is untouched, the firmware is untouched, and three vehicles differ by one float. This
decision is what keeps the change small enough to verify.

D4 TRANSIT DECONFLICTION MECHANISM
Options. (a) closed-loop ground-commanded avoidance; (b) altitude stratification during run-in; (c) strict temporal sequencing,
one vehicle at a time; (d) crossing-free slot assignment.
Decision: (b) + (d) as the primary mechanism, (c) as an operator-selectable conservative mode, (a) rejected.
Why (a) is rejected, precisely. The loop would enforce a metre-class separation criterion using positions known to
roughly ±1–2.5 m per vehicle, sampled at telemetry rate, transported over a link with variable latency, actuated by a
mode change with an ACK round trip. The loop delay is comparable to the time a 15 m/s vehicle needs to traverse the
entire keepout volume. Such a controller does not add safety; it adds a failure mode that activates precisely when the
link is degraded — that is, when the vehicles are already least supervised. STRATUM will not command avoidance
manoeuvres.
Options (b) and (d) are free, deterministic, and verifiable before the vehicles leave the ground. §7 develops them.
D5 SLOT ASSIGNMENT POLICY
Decision: STRATUM proposes; the operator disposes. The proposal is the cyclic-order-preserving assignment
that minimises total run-in path length. Any override is permitted; an override that induces a path crossing is
flagged, not blocked.
"Optimum" was undefined in the original objectives. This decision defines it as minimum total path length subject to a
no-crossing constraint — the objective that also happens to buy deconfliction. At n = 3 the assignment problem is six
permutations; enumeration is exact and costs nothing, so no solver is introduced.
The no-crossing constraint has a clean geometric basis: if the cyclic angular order of the vehicles' launch positions about
the target matches the cyclic order of their assigned slot bearings, straight-line run-ins do not cross.
D6 SEPARATION ENFORCEMENT SEMANTICS
Decision: the separation function is an advisory monitor that may recommend and, on operator authorisation,
command HOLD. It never commands a manoeuvre. The 1 m bubble is retained as the hard body keepout but is
not the operative criterion.
§7.5 derives the operative criterion as an uncertainty-inflated separation minimum and moves its enforcement from
runtime to slot-placement time in the wizard. Deconfliction becomes a constraint in the user interface, not a controller in
the air. A constraint that has already succeeded on the ground cannot fail in flight.

D7 ARRIVAL SYNCHRONISATION
Decision: v1 achieves time coherence by staggered commit — an open-loop schedule computed from path length
and nominal cruise speed, closed by observation of actual arrival skew. v2 adds post-activation
DO_CHANGE_SPEED trim per finding F4.
Whether simultaneous arrival is a requirement at all is still open (§11.2). Staggered commit costs nothing and delivers
coherence to within the accuracy of the cruise-speed estimate — a few seconds over a 500 m run-in. If a tighter number
is operationally required, F4 supplies the mechanism, and the ordering constraint (speed after mode activation, never
before) is recorded here so that it is not discovered in flight test.
D8 AUTHORITY AND FAILSAFE POSTURE
Decision: an orchestrated mission cannot reach ARMED until STRATUM has read back and verified a required
failsafe parameter block on every participating vehicle. Verification failure blocks the mission and names the
offending vehicle and parameter.
Per finding F5 the defaults are wrong for this mission. The mechanism — a gate, not a hope — is the decision; the
values are ratified in flight test.

vehicle id, so two Daggers shipped as delivered will present as one vehicle — the second simply never appears. This is a
silent failure that looks like a radio problem and is not one. It belongs in the production build and acceptance-test
procedure, not in software.

4 Entity model and component architecture
4.1 Entity model

agreement with the target. Second, SeparationMonitor is the only entity with visibility across agents — separation is an
emergent property of the ensemble, and only the component that observes the ensemble may make a separation claim. Nothing
else in the system is permitted to assert one.

6.6 Re-slotting
An ON_STATION vehicle is in Hold, not Standoff, so re-slotting repeats the full commit sequence with the new bearing. A
vehicle still in RUN_IN may be re-slotted by sending a fresh 31010 alone — standoff.cpp:117 restarts the staged
manoeuvre from phase 1 on any new setpoint — but that replays the in-place yaw, which reads as abrupt to an operator
watching the video feed. Prefer re-slotting from Hold; permit it from run-in.

7 Deconfliction architecture
7.1 Reframing the problem
The 1 m bubble was specified as a station-keeping guard. The geometry says it is not needed there. Chord separation between
two slots on a ring of radius R separated by angle θ is 2R·sin(θ/2).
Ring radius R θ = 120° (even) θ = 30° θ = 10° θ = 1°
50 m 86.6 m 25.9 m 8.7 m 0.87 m
100 m 173.2 m 51.8 m 17.4 m 1.75 m
300 m 519.6 m 155.3 m 52.3 m 5.24 m
THE CONCLUSION THIS FORCES
The terminal state is deconflicted by ring geometry. For two vehicles to approach the keepout volume on a 300 m ring they
would have to be placed less than one degree apart. The conflict lives entirely in the transit phase, and specifically near the
launch point, where three vehicles may depart from within tens of metres of one another. Any architecture that spends its effort
on station-keeping separation is solving the wrong problem.

B Closing argument
Because PX4 owns the standoff geometry, STRATUM does not need a trajectory generator; it needs a scheduler
and a constraint. Because the firmware latches the run-in altitude at mode activation, vertical deconfliction is
available for the price of one climb command. And because the ring geometry separates the terminal stations by
hundreds of metres, the 1 m bubble was guarding the one phase of flight that was never in danger.
The scan changed three things about this design, and each change made it smaller. That is usually the sign of an architecture
converging on the grain of the system rather than fighting it. The remaining decisions in §3 are then largely about restraint: not
streaming setpoints, not commanding avoidance, not re-solving the ring under fire, not refactoring a working single-vehicle
path to accommodate a new one.
The residual risk is not in the flight geometry. It is in the failsafe parameter block, where the stock defaults would have three
vehicles making independent, uncoordinated decisions at exactly the moment the ground station stops watching. That is why
D8 is a gate rather than a recommendation, and why the preflight check reads back parameters instead of trusting them.
Decisions dictate destiny.
