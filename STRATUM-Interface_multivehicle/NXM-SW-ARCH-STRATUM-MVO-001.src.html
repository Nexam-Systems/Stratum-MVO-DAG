1Purpose, scope and definitions

An architecture is a set of decisions. Everything else is consequence. This document records nine decisions for coordinating three Dagger air vehicles onto a standoff ring, the evidence from both code baselines that forced them, and the five rulings still outstanding.

1.1Purpose

This document defines the architecture of a multi-vehicle orchestration subsystem residing inside STRATUM-Desktop. The subsystem coordinates up to three Dagger air vehicles onto distinct stations on a standoff ring about a single operator-designated target. It is written to be reviewed, argued with, and then built from; §11 lists the questions that must be answered before construction begins.

1.2Scope

In scope: target designation, ring definition, slot assignment, launch and transit sequencing, arrival detection, separation assurance, and degraded-mode behaviour for a three-vehicle ensemble. Out of scope, deliberately and explicitly: multiple simultaneous targets; fixed-wing standoff geometry; inter-vehicle datalinks; and — pending the ruling in §11.5 — any transition from an orchestrated standoff into a terminal engagement.

1.3Definitions

Terms are fixed here and used with these meanings throughout. A term defined here is never used loosely elsewhere in the document.

TermDefinition
TargetA single geodetic point designated by the operator. One per mission. Primitive.
Standoff ringThe horizontal circle of radius R centred on the target at height H above home. Derived, never stored independently — it therefore cannot disagree with the target.
Ring slotA station on the ring, identified by bearing θ from target to hold point (0 = North, clockwise). One vehicle per slot; one slot per vehicle.
Transit levelThe altitude at which a vehicle performs its horizontal run-in. Distinct from the slot's hold height, and the basis of vertical deconfliction.
AgentSTRATUM's per-vehicle orchestration object: one Vehicle*, one slot, one state machine. An agent pins its vehicle for life and never reads activeVehicle.
CommitThe act of sending a vehicle its standoff geometry and switching it into the Standoff flight mode. The irreversible step in the sequence.
Separation bubbleThe keepout sphere carried by each vehicle. The nominal 1 m figure is retained as a hard body keepout but is not the operative criterion; §7.5 derives what is.
Deconfliction by constructionSeparation guaranteed by geometry and schedule chosen before flight, rather than by a runtime avoidance controller. The organising principle of §7.
TradespaceThe set of feasible architectures spanned by the decisions in §3, together with the consequences each choice imposes.

1.4System context

The system of interest sits between one operator and three air vehicles. It has no peers, no external interfaces beyond the existing MAVLink transport, and no authority that the operator has not granted. Figure 1 fixes the boundary.

2The as-built baseline

An architecture built on an assumed baseline is a hypothesis. This section records what the two code baselines actually do, with references, because four of the nine decisions in §3 were changed by what the scan found.

2.1The single-vehicle control chain

The essential division of labour in the existing system is already correct, and it is not the one an outside reader would assume. STRATUM does not compute the standoff hold point. It transmits the target plus three scalars; the firmware translates the target, resolves the hold altitude against home, and computes the facing yaw itself.

2.2The firmware's geometry and staging

The Standoff mode decomposes the manoeuvre into four ordered phases rather than commanding one diagonal move. The rationale in the firmware documentation is sensor settling: a payload that must acquire the target benefits from turning, translating and changing height as discrete, observable steps. That decision, taken for a single vehicle and for a wholly different reason, is what makes vertical deconfliction free for three vehicles — see finding F3 and §7.3.

2.3What is already multi-vehicle

Everything below the QML layer. This is the most consequential finding of the scan, because it converts the work from a refactor into an addition — and a refactor of a working weapon-system C2 application is a risk that does not need to be taken.

2.4What is not

Only the Nexam FlyView QML singletons. Each is instantiated exactly once and bound to QGroundControl.multiVehicleManager.activeVehicle: StandoffController, EngagementController, the abort and vision overlays, the Set Standoff panel, every custom GuidedAction* button, and — visually — the single surveillance circle and target marker in FlyViewMap.qml:796–873.

Two components already do it correctly and serve as the internal precedent: TrackerRoiOverlay.qml and TargetTrackingOverlay.qml both take vehicle as a required property rather than reaching for the global. The architecture in §4 generalises that precedent rather than inventing a pattern.

2.5Five findings that dictate the architecture

Finding F1 — PX4 owns the hold-point geometry
On the wire, a three-vehicle ring mission is three single-vehicle standoffs that differ in param2 alone. This is the cheapest possible extension of a proven contract, and it removes the trajectory generator from STRATUM's requirements entirely.
Finding F2 — arrival is already observable
standoff.cpp:139 calls request_hold_mode() exactly once when the terminal pose settles. The vehicle therefore announces its own arrival as a nav_state 9 → 3 transition. No new telemetry, no vehicle-side change, no arrival estimator.
Finding F3 — the run-in flies at the altitude latched on activation
standoff.cpp:238 latches _approach_alt_amsl from the current global position at begin_sequence(), and APPROACH publishes that altitude. Climb each vehicle to its own transit level before commit and the run-ins are vertically separated with zero firmware change. Conditional on STDF_SEQ ∈ {1,3}.
Finding F4 — cruising speed is trimmable, but only after activation
on_activation calls reset_cruising_speed(), and APPROACH republishes its setpoint every cycle carrying cruising_speed = get_cruising_speed() (standoff.cpp:388). A DO_CHANGE_SPEED sent after nav_state == 9 is confirmed takes effect within one navigator cycle. Sent before, it is discarded.
Finding F5 — failsafe defaults are hostile to a ring
No custom mode has bespoke failsafe treatment; all four are bucketed with stock AUTO modes under the generic AutoModes exception bit (failsafe.cpp:504–542). With stock parameters: NAV_DLL_ACT = 0 — datalink loss triggers nothing; COM_RCL_EXCEPT = 0 with NAV_RCL_ACT = 2 — RC loss triggers RTL. Three vehicles executing independent, uncoordinated RTLs from three points on a ring is precisely the crossing pattern this subsystem exists to prevent.

3Architectural decisions

Nine decisions. Each states the option space, the choice, and the consequence the choice imposes. A decision without a stated consequence is a preference, not an architecture.

D1LOCUS OF COORDINATION
Options. (a) ground-side orchestration in STRATUM; (b) onboard distributed coordination between vehicles; (c) hybrid — ground planning, onboard deconfliction.
Decision: (a). STRATUM plans and supervises; PX4 executes. No inter-vehicle link, no onboard awareness of neighbours.

Rationale. Option (b) requires an inter-vehicle datalink, a shared world model and a distributed-consensus safety case; that is a programme, not a feature, and it is unjustified at n = 3. Option (c) requires firmware change to a stack whose own documentation records that it was statically reviewed but never compile-verified in the environment that produced it. Option (a) reuses a proven contract end to end.

Consequence. Ground-side coordination promotes the C2 link from a supervisory element to a mission-critical one. That consequence is paid off by D4: if separation is guaranteed by geometry rather than by supervision, losing the link degrades the mission without endangering the airframes. The link must not be load-bearing for safety. This is the single most important structural commitment in this document, and every later decision is checked against it.

D2COMMAND PRIMITIVE
Options. (a) reuse DO_STANDOFF (31010) + DO_SET_MODE per vehicle; (b) upload per-vehicle missions through MissionManager; (c) stream offboard setpoints from STRATUM.
Decision: (a), with (b) held in reserve for multi-leg routing.

Option (c) is rejected outright: streaming setpoints from a ground station makes the radio link an inner control loop, which fails D1's payoff condition immediately and converts every link dropout into a control-loop failure. Option (b) is over-engineering for a straight-line run-in, but the machinery exists, is already per-vehicle, and remains available if terrain or airspace ever forces routed approaches.

D3GEOMETRY BOUNDARY
Decision: STRATUM owns ring geometry and scheduling. PX4 owns hold-point geometry and approach staging. The boundary is the existing 31010 contract, unchanged.

STRATUM computes the ring from (target, R, H); the per-slot bearing θi; the per-vehicle transit level; and the commit schedule. It sends (target, R, θi, H) to vehicle i. Nothing else crosses the boundary.

Consequence. The wire contract is untouched, the firmware is untouched, and three vehicles differ by one float. This decision is what keeps the change small enough to verify.

D4TRANSIT DECONFLICTION MECHANISM
Options. (a) closed-loop ground-commanded avoidance; (b) altitude stratification during run-in; (c) strict temporal sequencing, one vehicle at a time; (d) crossing-free slot assignment.
Decision: (b) + (d) as the primary mechanism, (c) as an operator-selectable conservative mode, (a) rejected.

Why (a) is rejected, precisely. The loop would enforce a metre-class separation criterion using positions known to roughly ±1–2.5 m per vehicle, sampled at telemetry rate, transported over a link with variable latency, actuated by a mode change with an ACK round trip. The loop delay is comparable to the time a 15 m/s vehicle needs to traverse the entire keepout volume. Such a controller does not add safety; it adds a failure mode that activates precisely when the link is degraded — that is, when the vehicles are already least supervised. STRATUM will not command avoidance manoeuvres.

Options (b) and (d) are free, deterministic, and verifiable before the vehicles leave the ground. §7 develops them.

D5SLOT ASSIGNMENT POLICY
Decision: STRATUM proposes; the operator disposes. The proposal is the cyclic-order-preserving assignment that minimises total run-in path length. Any override is permitted; an override that induces a path crossing is flagged, not blocked.

"Optimum" was undefined in the original objectives. This decision defines it as minimum total path length subject to a no-crossing constraint — the objective that also happens to buy deconfliction. At n = 3 the assignment problem is six permutations; enumeration is exact and costs nothing, so no solver is introduced.

The no-crossing constraint has a clean geometric basis: if the cyclic angular order of the vehicles' launch positions about the target matches the cyclic order of their assigned slot bearings, straight-line run-ins do not cross.

D6SEPARATION ENFORCEMENT SEMANTICS
Decision: the separation function is an advisory monitor that may recommend and, on operator authorisation, command HOLD. It never commands a manoeuvre. The 1 m bubble is retained as the hard body keepout but is not the operative criterion.

§7.5 derives the operative criterion as an uncertainty-inflated separation minimum and moves its enforcement from runtime to slot-placement time in the wizard. Deconfliction becomes a constraint in the user interface, not a controller in the air. A constraint that has already succeeded on the ground cannot fail in flight.

D7ARRIVAL SYNCHRONISATION
Decision: v1 achieves time coherence by staggered commit — an open-loop schedule computed from path length and nominal cruise speed, closed by observation of actual arrival skew. v2 adds post-activation DO_CHANGE_SPEED trim per finding F4.

Whether simultaneous arrival is a requirement at all is still open (§11.2). Staggered commit costs nothing and delivers coherence to within the accuracy of the cruise-speed estimate — a few seconds over a 500 m run-in. If a tighter number is operationally required, F4 supplies the mechanism, and the ordering constraint (speed after mode activation, never before) is recorded here so that it is not discovered in flight test.

D8AUTHORITY AND FAILSAFE POSTURE
Decision: an orchestrated mission cannot reach ARMED until STRATUM has read back and verified a required failsafe parameter block on every participating vehicle. Verification failure blocks the mission and names the offending vehicle and parameter.

Per finding F5 the defaults are wrong for this mission. The mechanism — a gate, not a hope — is the decision; the values are ratified in flight test.

The STDF_SEQ row is not a preference. It is a coupling between a parameter documented as a sensor-settling convenience and a property the safety argument depends on. It must be verified, never assumed.

D9VEHICLE IDENTITY PROVISIONING
Decision: MAV_SYS_ID is a per-airframe configuration item under production control. STRATUM detects a colliding fleet and refuses the mission.

MAV_SYS_ID defaults to 1 and is unmanaged in the fork; nothing auto-assigns it. MultiVehicleManager dedupes by vehicle id, so two Daggers shipped as delivered will present as one vehicle — the second simply never appears. This is a silent failure that looks like a radio problem and is not one. It belongs in the production build and acceptance-test procedure, not in software.

4Entity model and component architecture

4.1Entity model

Two properties of this model carry weight. First, the standoff ring is derived and never stored, so it cannot drift out of agreement with the target. Second, SeparationMonitor is the only entity with visibility across agents — separation is an emergent property of the ensemble, and only the component that observes the ensemble may make a separation claim. Nothing else in the system is permitted to assert one.

5Data flow architecture

5.1Command path

The command path for one agent is shown in full in Figure 7. Two properties are worth stating before reading it. Every step is gated on an acknowledgement or an observed state, so the agent never assumes a command landed. And every element of the path below the agent already exists and is already per-vehicle, so three agents running this path concurrently contend for nothing.

5.2Telemetry path and the separation monitor

The monitor consumes three streams per vehicle and produces one advisory. It is the only component permitted to reason across vehicles, and it is deliberately given no actuation authority — per D6, its output is information for the operator, not a command for an airframe.

5.3Wire contract — unchanged

No message, field or identifier changes. The full contract is reproduced in Appendix A for reference during implementation; the summary is that a three-vehicle mission uses the same six message types a one-vehicle mission uses today, addressed to three system identifiers instead of one.

6.1Operator workflow

The wizard exists to make the plan reviewable before it is irreversible. Everything up to page 5 is editable and costs nothing; page 5 puts vehicles in the air. The constraint that guarantees terminal separation is enforced on page 3, on the ground, where failing it is an inconvenience rather than an incident.

6.3Agent state machine

6.4Commit schedule and arrival coherence

Commit is staggered so that arrivals converge. STRATUM estimates each run-in duration from path length and nominal cruise speed, then delays each agent's commit by the difference. The schedule is open-loop; the skew is measured and reported, and that measurement is what informs whether the v2 speed trim is worth building.

6.5Arrival predicate

The 9 → 3 transition alone is not sufficient, because a vehicle can reach Hold by other routes — operator action, or a failsafe. ON_STATION therefore requires all three of the following, at negligible cost:

1. nav_state transitioned 9 → 3 (the firmware's own arrival announcement) 2. horizontal distance to the computed slot coordinate < NAV_ACC_RAD 3. | altitudeAMSL − (home_alt + H) | < altitude acceptance radius

6.6Re-slotting

An ON_STATION vehicle is in Hold, not Standoff, so re-slotting repeats the full commit sequence with the new bearing. A vehicle still in RUN_IN may be re-slotted by sending a fresh 31010 alone — standoff.cpp:117 restarts the staged manoeuvre from phase 1 on any new setpoint — but that replays the in-place yaw, which reads as abrupt to an operator watching the video feed. Prefer re-slotting from Hold; permit it from run-in.

7Deconfliction architecture

7.1Reframing the problem

The 1 m bubble was specified as a station-keeping guard. The geometry says it is not needed there. Chord separation between two slots on a ring of radius R separated by angle θ is 2R·sin(θ/2).

Ring radius Rθ = 120° (even)θ = 30°θ = 10°θ = 1°

The conclusion this forces
The terminal state is deconflicted by ring geometry. For two vehicles to approach the keepout volume on a 300 m ring they would have to be placed less than one degree apart. The conflict lives entirely in the transit phase, and specifically near the launch point, where three vehicles may depart from within tens of metres of one another. Any architecture that spends its effort on station-keeping separation is solving the wrong problem.

7.2Layer 1 — structural, at slot-placement time

The wizard enforces θ_min = 2·asin(R_req / 2R) as a drag constraint on page 3. Figure 16 shows how weak that constraint is in practice at any operationally sensible ring radius — which is the point. The constraint costs the operator nothing and removes an entire failure class.

7.3Layer 2 — vertical stratification during transit

This is the workhorse, and it costs one extra climb command per vehicle. Per finding F3, the APPROACH phase flies at the altitude latched when Standoff activates. Climb each vehicle to its own level before commit and the run-ins are vertically separated whatever their lateral paths do. The HEIGHT phase then brings each vehicle to the common hold altitude only once it is over its own slot, by which point Layer 1 guarantees the lateral separation.

7.4Layer 3 — assignment and schedule

Cyclic-order-preserving assignment (D5) removes lateral crossings for straight-line run-ins; staggered commit (D7) separates departures in time. Neither costs anything, and both are computed in the planner before commit.

7.5Layer 4 — the monitor, and what the bubble actually is

The operative separation minimum is not a constant. It is a function of what the system actually knows about where the vehicles are:

R_req(i,j) = R_body + k · ( hAcc_i + hAcc_j ) R_body = 1 m hard body keepout — the nominal "phantom bubble" hAcc = reported GNSS horizontal accuracy, per vehicle (already in STRATUM: vehicle.gpsFactGroup.horizontalAccuracy, from GPS_RAW_INT h_acc — added by commit 6df2bc3 for a display purpose) k = 2 for approximately 95% confidence

8Failure and degraded modes

A degraded-mode table is the honest test of an architecture, because it is where optimistic assumptions surface. The organising claim below is that the C2 link is not load-bearing for safety after commit — and the table is where that claim is either kept or broken.

8.1The one entry that is not self-closing

Battery failsafe is uncoordinated by design: no mode-aware deferral exists in the firmware, and none is proposed. A critical-battery event will act on whatever nav state the vehicle is in, independently of the other two. The architectural response is not to suppress it — suppressing a battery failsafe to preserve a formation is an inversion of priorities — but to make it visible early. STRATUM must display remaining endurance per agent against the planned run-in and hold duration, so that the operator sees it coming rather than discovering it.

9Firmware deltas and implementation staging

9.1Firmware deltas required

For version 1: none
The architecture rides entirely on the existing 31010 contract, the existing Standoff mode, the existing request_hold_mode() hand-off, and the existing STDF_SEQ staging. Everything that must change is either a STRATUM addition or a parameter value. This is the strongest single argument for the design, and it is the reason the verification burden is tractable.

Non-blocking firmware candidates, recorded here so they are not rediscovered later:

  • A sequence or echo identifier on standoff_setpoint would let STRATUM confirm which setpoint a vehicle is flying, not merely that one was accepted. Useful when re-slotting under a degraded link, where an unacknowledged re-command and a stale one are currently indistinguishable.
  • The asymmetry between EngagementDive — which, on target loss, levels and waits under the same nav state with no self-abort — and VisionEngagement, which self-aborts to nav_state 16, should be resolved before any multi-vehicle engagement is contemplated. It is out of scope for v1 and blocking for §11.5.

11Open decisions requiring ruling

These are not gaps. They are the points at which the architecture is deliberately parameterised pending an operational answer, and each is stated with the default the design will take if no ruling is given.

§QuestionDefault if unruledWhat changes with the answer
11.1Common or per-slot hold height?Common H, distinct transit levelsThe wire contract already carries z per vehicle, so per-slot heights are trivially supported — but they change the sensor geometry and the endurance picture.
11.2Is arrival skew a requirement with a number?Best-effort via staggered commit; skew measured and reportedIf "all three on station within T seconds" is real, state T and the v2 speed trim moves from deferred into S3.
11.3Launch topology — single pad or dispersed?Single pad; departure separation in time as well as altitudeDispersed launch points make the cyclic-order assignment materially more useful and relax the departure schedule.

It should be scoped in deliberately or excluded explicitly. It should not arrive by accident.

BClosing argument

Because PX4 owns the standoff geometry, STRATUM does not need a trajectory generator; it needs a scheduler and a constraint. Because the firmware latches the run-in altitude at mode activation, vertical deconfliction is available for the price of one climb command. And because the ring geometry separates the terminal stations by hundreds of metres, the 1 m bubble was guarding the one phase of flight that was never in danger.

The scan changed three things about this design, and each change made it smaller. That is usually the sign of an architecture converging on the grain of the system rather than fighting it. The remaining decisions in §3 are then largely about restraint: not streaming setpoints, not commanding avoidance, not re-solving the ring under fire, not refactoring a working single-vehicle path to accommodate a new one.

The residual risk is not in the flight geometry. It is in the failsafe parameter block, where the stock defaults would have three vehicles making independent, uncoordinated decisions at exactly the moment the ground station stops watching. That is why D8 is a gate rather than a recommendation, and why the preflight check reads back parameters instead of trusting them.

Decisions dictate destiny.
